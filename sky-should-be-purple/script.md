## Fact check

- **The sky isn't reflecting the ocean.** It's just as blue over deserts and far inland. The sea partly looks blue *because* it reflects the sky, and because water absorbs red light.
- **Rayleigh scattering** goes as 1/wavelength⁴ (Lord Rayleigh, 1871). Blue at about 450 nm vs red at about 700 nm: (700/450)⁴ ≈ 5.9, so **about 6×**. Violet at about 400 nm: (700/400)⁴ ≈ 9.4, so **about 9×**.
- **Why we don't see purple:** sunlight has less violet than blue (the solar spectrum peaks around 500 nm), some of it is absorbed high in the atmosphere, and our eyes are much less sensitive to violet. The blue-heavy mix of scattered light reads as sky blue.
- **Sunsets:** at the horizon, sunlight passes through roughly 38 times as much air as when the Sun is overhead. Most of the short wavelengths are scattered out of the beam, leaving orange and red.
- **The Moon:** it has no real atmosphere (only an extremely thin exosphere), so nothing scatters sunlight. The daytime sky there is black.
- "Isn't the real colour" is a simplification: the scattered light's spectrum leans towards violet and blue, and "blue" is how our eyes and brain sum it up.
