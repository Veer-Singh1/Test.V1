## Voiceover text

In 1973, two thousand workers in Durban did something illegal. They stopped making bricks. It was the ninth of January, at the Coronation Brick and Tile works. They earned under nine rand a week, and they marched to a football stadium to demand more. But Black workers had no union, and striking was against the law. Then the company offered one rand fifty more. They said no, and got just over two rand. Within weeks, six thousand Frame textile workers walked out too. By March, more than sixty thousand workers at over a hundred Durban firms had downed tools. You see, a boss can fire one worker. But no factory runs without all of them. So the government set up an inquiry. In 1979, it said Black unions should be recognised. It all started with two thousand people who stopped making bricks.

## Left out on purpose

- **"100,000 workers".** Your cheat sheet uses this number and one source agrees (Elitsha), but most sources say about 60,000. The script uses the safer number. **In your exam, use the figure your teacher gave.**
- **The original demand (R20 or R30).** Sources disagree.
- **The final wage of R11,50.** It's stated twice, but R8,97 + R2,07 doesn't add up to R11,50, so I left it out.
- **Municipal workers (3,000 rising to 16,000).** Only one source gives these numbers.
- **African union membership growing from 70,000 to 300,000 (1979-83).** Only one source gives this.
- **COSATU (1985).** It's a true link, but it pushed the "So" beat too early. It can go in a second version if you like.

## Wording to watch

- "So the government set up an inquiry." The Wiehahn Commission (1977) also responded to the 1976 Soweto uprising, so "so" simplifies the cause. The script never claims the strikes were the only reason.
- "Should be recognised." It was a recommendation, and unions still had to register with the state. That's why the script says "said ... should be", not "made legal".
- Say "nineteen seventy-three", "one rand fifty" and "nineteen seventy-nine".
- "Under nine rand" = R8,97. Don't round it up to "nine rand".
