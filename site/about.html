About · Est. 2019

One sack. One popcorn machine.

Manifest started in a Glenwood garage with a 60 kg sack of Ethiopian green coffee and a second-hand hot-air popcorn popper that roasted 80 grams at a time. The neighbours put up with the smoke. Seven years later we roast on a 15 kg drum in an old bonded warehouse by the harbour, a short walk from where the coffee comes ashore.

Est. 2019Glenwood · Durban

Ship's log

How we got here

Every ship keeps a log. Ours is less nautical and has more burnt coffee in it.

  1. 2019March

    First crack, in a garage in Glenwood

    Zanele and Kiran roast 80 g batches in a popcorn popper and sell them at a Saturday market, in brown paper bags with the roast date written on in koki. People come back the next week, and then the week after.

    Conditions: humid. Smoke alarm: disabled (sorry, landlord).

  2. 2020April

    Lockdown, by bicycle

    The market closes, so we deliver on two bicycles to forty-odd households around Glenwood and Berea. The popper retires and a 1 kg drum roaster takes over the garage.

    Conditions: uphill, both ways. Calves: impressive.

  3. 2021August

    Our first café

    A café on Florida Road puts our coffee on its grinder. Sipho comes in to train their baristas for a week and ends up staying for good.

    Conditions: one café, one grinder, many nerves.

  4. 2023February

    Moving to the harbour

    We sign a lease on a unit in an old bonded warehouse off Maydon Wharf Road. Being this close to the port means we can collect green coffee the same week it clears customs, which is the whole idea.

    Conditions: forklift traffic. Gulls: opinionated.

  5. 2025June

    Our first direct lot

    Kiran spends three weeks visiting washing stations around Lake Kivu. We buy a whole lot from a station in Nyamasheke and publish exactly what we paid for it.

    Conditions: rainy season ending. Cupping scores: very good.

  6. 2026November

    A bigger drum and an open door

    The 15 kg roaster is installed, the front counter opens six days a week, and Saturday cuppings are now free and open to everyone. The log continues.

    Conditions: summer's here. Iced coffee: on.

What we believe

Terms & conditions of carriage

A bill of lading comes with pages of small print. Ours is four clauses long, and we stick to it.

  1. §1

    Fresh is a date, not an adjective.

    Every bag shows the day it was roasted. We don't sell coffee more than five weeks after that date. Older bags become cold brew at the counter.

  2. §2

    We say what we paid.

    For every single origin we publish the price paid at origin next to that week's commodity price. If we can't find out what the farmer received, we say that too.

    For example, Lot 0415 from Rwanda: we paid 2.6 times the commodity price.

  3. §3

    Nobody gets judged at the counter.

    Cappuccino with three sugars? Rooibos instead? That's fine by us. We'll happily tell you about the Kenyan, but only if you ask.

  4. §4

    The harbour is our neighbour.

    We hire people who live nearby where we can. The roasting chaff goes to community gardens in Clairwood and the spent grounds go to a worm farm in Merebank.
