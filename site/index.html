The voyage

Every bean on our shelf crossed the Indian Ocean.

Coffee grows in a belt across East Africa, and most of it reaches South Africa through Durban. Keep scrolling to follow one season's cargo down the coast to our roaster.

  1. Day 00Djibouti11°36′N 43°09′E

    Ethiopia comes aboard

    Ethiopia has no coastline, so its coffee travels by rail from Addis to Djibouti before it goes to sea. Every season we buy from the south: Guji, Sidama, sometimes Yirgacheffe when the cupping table agrees.

    • Guji · washed
    • Sidama · natural

  2. Day 06Mombasa4°03′S 39°40′E

    Kenya, and a little Uganda

    Juicy SL28 and SL34 from Nyeri's red volcanic soil. A truck also brings naturals down from Mount Elgon in Uganda, which go into our house espresso.

    • Nyeri AA · washed
    • Mt Elgon · natural

  3. Day 09Dar es Salaam6°49′S 39°17′E

    Peaberry and the long road from Kivu

    Tanzanian peaberry from Mbeya, plus Rwandan lots that have already travelled 1,400 km by road from the shores of Lake Kivu just to reach the sea.

    • Mbeya peaberry
    • Kivu · red bourbon

  4. Day 14Beira19°50′S 34°50′E

    The wildcard

    Malawi's Misuku Hills grow tiny, wonderful lots that hardly anyone in South Africa roasts. Some seasons we get a few sacks and some seasons we don't. If you see it on the list, don't wait.

    • Misuku · washed geisha (seasonal)

  5. Day 19Durban · ZADUR29°52′S 31°01′E

    Customs cleared. Drum's hot.

    The container is cleared at the port, the sacks are trucked a few hundred metres to Maydon Wharf, and by day 21 the first batch is in the drum. Most coffee in South Africa sits for months before roasting. Ours is roasted within days of landing.

    • Roasted Tue & Thu
    • Delivered Wed & Fri
