## Fact check

- **Spelling:** the term is **Mie scattering**, after Gustav Mie (1908). The captions and stamp use the real spelling so viewers can search it; the voice says "Mee", which is correct.
- **Air molecules vs light:** N₂ and O₂ molecules are about 0.3 nm across, roughly 1,000 times smaller than a visible light wave (400–700 nm). That's the Rayleigh regime, where blue scatters far more than red (the previous video).
- **Cloud droplets are huge by comparison:** typical cloud droplets are about 10–20 µm across, so at least 20 light waves (about 0.5 µm each) fit across one. For particles that large, scattering is nearly the same for every colour, and the mix looks white.
- **"Billions" of droplets** is an understatement: a cubic metre of cloud holds roughly 100 million to a billion droplets, so a whole cloud has trillions and more.
- **Storm clouds look grey from below** because they are optically very thick. Most sunlight is scattered back out of the top (thick clouds reflect up to about 90%), and only a small fraction makes it through to the base. "<10%" is an illustrative value for a thick storm cloud, and the exact number depends on thickness. The bigger droplets in storm clouds also absorb a little.
