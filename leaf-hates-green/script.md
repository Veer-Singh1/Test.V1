## Fact check

- **White light contains every visible colour.** Sunlight and white lamps have a continuous visible spectrum, and a prism separates it (Newton, *Opticks*, 1704).
- **Selective absorption** is the textbook term for why objects have colour. A pigment absorbs some wavelengths and reflects or scatters the rest, and we see the light that reaches the eye. A red dye mostly absorbs blue-green light and reflects red. (Simplification: real dyes reflect a band of colours around red, not a single wavelength.)
- **Chlorophyll eats red and blue.** Chlorophyll *a* and *b* absorb most strongly in the blue (about 430–470 nm) and the red (about 640–680 nm), and only weakly in the green, which is why leaves look green.
- **"90 % of red + blue light absorbed"**: a typical green leaf absorbs roughly 90 % or more of the blue and red light that hits it (e.g. Terashima et al., *Plant & Cell Physiology* 50, 684 (2009)). Leaves absorb a lot of green too (roughly 70–80 %), but they reflect and transmit more green than red or blue, and that leftover is the colour we see. "Green is the leftover" is a simplification, but it's true.
- **No light, no colour.** Colour is how we perceive the light that reaches our eyes. In total darkness nothing reflects anything, so nothing looks any colour.

## Decisions

- **Colour exists only as light.** The kit is black and white with one accent, but this topic *is* colour. So the spectrum colours only ever appear as light (beams, photons, arrows) or on an object while its reflected light reaches an eye. Lights off means everything goes grey. This keeps the kit's look and makes the idea visible.
- Volt Blue is used twice, both times for "energy": absorbed light in the shirt at the named concept, and chlorophyll turning red and blue light into energy.
- The world is stacked top to bottom (leaf, then the crowd, then the objects), so the pull-back for "turn off the lights" fills a 9:16 frame.
