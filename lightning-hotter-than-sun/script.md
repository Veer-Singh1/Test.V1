## Fact check

- **~30,000 °C:** a lightning channel briefly reaches about 30,000 K (NOAA and the US National Weather Service give up to about 50,000 °F, roughly 27,700 °C). That's about **five times** the Sun's visible surface (the photosphere, about 5,500 °C / 5,800 K).
- **Fine print:** it's hotter than the Sun's *surface*. The Sun's core is about 15 million °C, and the script says "the surface of the Sun", so it's accurate.
- **Plasma** is gas so hot that electrons are torn off its atoms (ionised). "Its atoms get ripped apart" is a simplification; the picture shows electrons being ripped off, which is what actually happens.
- **Thunder:** the channel heats in microseconds, the pressure jumps, and the air expands faster than sound, making a shock wave. Within metres it slows into an ordinary sound wave. "A sonic boom made by lightning" is a fair simplification, since both are shock waves. The long rumble you usually hear comes from different parts of a kilometres-long channel, which are different distances from you, plus echoes.
- **Flash to bang:** sound travels about 343 m/s, so 3 s ≈ 1 km.
