## Narration (plain text)

To reach orbit, you don't just go up. You go sideways… really, really fast.
A rocket throws mass out the back. Push exhaust down, and it pushes you up. No air needed: it even works in empty space.
The catch? You have to carry all that propellant with you. Roughly 90% of a rocket's mass on the launch pad is propellant.
So rockets come in stages. When a stage runs dry, it's dropped. No hauling empty tanks. Lighter rocket, more speed from the same fuel.
Newton imagined a cannon on a mountain. Fire faster, and the ball lands farther away. Fire fast enough, and the ground curves away as fast as the ball falls. It never lands. That's orbit.
Low Earth orbit needs about 28,000 km/h. That's about thirty times faster than an airliner.
So orbit isn't up. It's sideways: falling, and always missing.
